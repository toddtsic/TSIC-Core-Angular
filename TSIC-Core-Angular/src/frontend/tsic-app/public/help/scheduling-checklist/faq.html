Scheduling Checklist

Work the steps top to bottom.

Each step links to the screen that turns it green, and every one of those screens has a back arrow to here. When pools, play dates and timeslots are green, Build unlocks.

Where did the rest of the Scheduling menu go?

It's all here. The menu used to list eleven scheduling screens with no indication of which ones you still needed; now there's one Scheduling Checklist item, and this page routes you to everything — steps at the top, tools below. Every screen it opens has a back arrow to return here.

Why is Build Schedule locked?

One of the three steps it depends on isn't finished, and the row names them: "Locked until complete: pools, play dates, timeslots". The most common blocker is teams still sitting in the Unassigned pool — those teams can never be placed in a schedule, so the build stays locked until every age group is fully pooled.

What does the detail line under a step mean?

It's the live list of what's missing. "2029: 12 of 59" under Pools means that age group still has 12 unpooled teams out of 59. "No play dates: 2030" means that age group has no game dates. "4 pools without timeslots — 2030: White, Red" names the individual pools. Fix exactly what's named and the step turns green.

Play dates and timeslots are one step. Which is which?

They're set on the same screen but at different grains. Play dates belong to the age group — 2030 plays Saturday and Sunday. Timeslots belong to the pool — you set up 2030 Red, then 2030 White. Step 3 reports both, so a green age group can still show individual pools missing timeslots.

Pairings shows missing pool sizes. Do I have to fix that?

No — pairings are informational and never block the build. Any missing pool size is generated automatically when the build runs. Open the Pairings screen only if you want to review or customize who-plays-whom beforehand.

It mentions divisions without a game guarantee. Is that blocking me?

No. If a division has no guarantee set, the builder works one out from the pairing table and writes it back. The note exists so you know it's being derived rather than chosen — if the derived number isn't what your event promises, set it explicitly on the hub's Build Rules tab before you build.

I don't see a Bracket Seeds step.

Then this event has no bracket games. The step only appears when there are playoff games to seed — showing a permanently green check for work that never applied would be a hollow claim. Bracket games are created during the build from championship pairings; once they exist, the step appears.

Bracket Seeds is red but I've seeded everything I can see.

A slot counts as covered if it has a seed ("Pool A #1") or an advancement feed — meaning it's filled by the winner or loser of an earlier bracket game. Later-round slots are usually fed, not seeded. The step lists the uncovered slots by age group and slot label, so open Bracket Seeds and look for exactly those.

Why are the Tools grayed out?

The Tools all operate on games — Master Schedule, Rescheduler, Schedule Viewer, QA Results and the rest — so they unlock once the schedule has been built. Until then the steps are the whole job.

QA Results opened the Schedule hub instead of its own page. Is that right?

Yes. QA Results and Master Schedule are segments of the Schedule hub, so their tools open the hub with that segment already selected — one door, one set of chrome. Everything on those screens works the same as it always did; the exports and stats are all still there.

A step went from green back to red. What happened?

The checklist reads live data, so something changed underneath it — new teams registered into an age group and landed in Unassigned, or a pool was added without timeslots. That's the point of it: it stays accurate all event long rather than recording that you once finished a step.

Tip: for a returning event, the checklist is the fastest way to confirm what carried forward from last year. A glance tells you whether dates and timeslots are already green before you spend time in the configuration screens.
