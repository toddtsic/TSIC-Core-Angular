Scheduling Checklist

This is the front door to scheduling — the one Scheduling item in the menu, and the page every other scheduling screen sends you back to. It is an ordered list of the work that has to happen before a schedule can be built, with live status read from your actual data. Each step names exactly what is still missing — which age groups, which pools, which teams — and links straight to the screen that fixes it.

Build stays locked until the steps above it are green.

Teams left in the Unassigned pool, age groups with no play dates, pools with no timeslots — the builder would silently skip all three. The checklist blocks the build rather than let it produce a schedule that quietly leaves teams out.

The steps, in order

1 · Assign Teams To Pools
Every team moves out of Unassigned into a real pool. The detail line counts the unpooled teams and names the age groups holding them — "2029: 12 of 59". Opens Pool Assignment.
2 · Pairings
Who plays whom, per pool size. Informational — it never blocks the build, because any missing pool size is generated automatically when the build runs. Visit it only to review or customize the pattern.
3 · Assign Timeslots
Two things on one screen: the dates each age group plays and the field timeslots each pool gets on those days. The detail line reports both — "No play dates: 2030" and "4 pools without timeslots — 2030: White, Red".
4 · Build Schedule
Unlocks once pools, play dates and timeslots are green, then opens the Schedule hub to run the build. Once games exist the row turns green and shows the game count.
5 · Bracket Seeds (only when the event has bracket games)
Every playoff slot needs either a seed ("Pool A #1") or an advancement feed (the winner of an earlier bracket game). The step counts the slots that have neither. It is hidden entirely on events with no bracket games.

Reading a step

  • The badge on the left is the step number while there's work left, and turns into a green check when the step is done.
  • The line under the title is live data, not boilerplate. If it names an age group or a pool, that is the thing standing between you and a build.
  • Build Schedule shows what is blocking it — "Locked until complete: pools, play dates" — so you never have to guess which step to go back to.
Game guarantees don't block the build.

If a division has no game guarantee set, the builder derives one from the pairing table and uses that. The Build step mentions the divisions in that position as a note, so you can set an explicit number on the hub's Build Rules tab if the derived one isn't what you want.

Tools — after the build

The Tools grid unlocks once games exist. These are the things you reach for over and over during an event; none of them is ever "finished", which is why none of them is a step:

  • Master Schedule — the whole event on one grid.
  • Rescheduler — move or swap individual games; rain-delay the whole day.
  • Schedule Viewer — browse games, enter scores, check standings and brackets.
  • Schedule Reports — printable game cards and team schedules.
  • QA Results — conflict checks to run before you publish.
  • Tournament Parking and Mobile Scorers — game-day logistics.

Master Schedule and QA Results open inside the Schedule hub on their own segment, so the chrome around them stays the same wherever you came from. Every other tool opens on its own page with a back arrow to this checklist.

After a build, the header tells you what you built

Once games exist a stat band appears under the title: the date span and number of play days, the fields in use, and the divisions scheduled. The game count stays in the badge next to the page title. Before the build there is no band — readiness is the only status that matters, and that's the list itself.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a partially-red step as "close enough". One age group with unpooled teams blocks the entire build. The step names it — fix exactly that.
  • Chasing the Pairings step. It is informational by design. Missing pool sizes are generated at build time; it will never unlock anything.
  • Assuming last year carried forward. A returning event often has dates and timeslots already seeded from the prior year — the checklist tells you whether they actually landed. Trust the status, not the memory of setting it up.
  • Not coming back. Pools change during registration. Re-check the checklist after roster or pool changes, not just before the first build.
