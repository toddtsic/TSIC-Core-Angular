E-Mail Troubleshooter

Two tabs, two questions.

Suppression List asks "is this address blocked?" Investigate asks "we sent a real test — did it get through, and if not, whose side failed?"

What does "Suppressed" actually mean?

The email provider (Amazon SES) has this address on its do-not-send list, usually because a past message hard-bounced (mailbox didn't exist) or the recipient marked one as spam. While suppressed, the address receives nothing — and no error is shown anywhere, which is why it looks like silence.

Is it safe to remove an address from suppression?

Yes, when the person is asking for their mail — that's exactly what removal is for (a fixed mailbox, a mistaken spam click). If the address is genuinely dead, the next send will bounce and it will simply end up suppressed again; no harm done.

The Investigate test says "Recipient side" but they still see nothing. Now what?

The message left our system and their provider accepted it. Have them check spam/junk and promotions folders, search for the sender address, and add it to their contacts/safe list. School and workplace domains sometimes quarantine mail before the inbox — their IT can release it.

Why include my own address in an Investigate run?

It's a control. If your copy arrives and the suspect address's doesn't, you've proven the sending side works and isolated the problem to their mailbox — a much stronger answer than "we sent it, trust us."

Does the Investigate test send a real email?

Yes — a short, clearly-labeled test message is actually dispatched to each address you enter. Warn the person to expect it so they can confirm arrival (or check spam for it).

How is this different from the Email Log?

The Email Log is history — proof of what was sent and to whom. The Troubleshooter is diagnosis — live checks against the email provider right now. Use the log to confirm a past send; use this tool to find out why the next one won't arrive.

Tip: players can self-serve this. The Check My Email page (avatar menu) lets a family check and un-block their own addresses without contacting you.
