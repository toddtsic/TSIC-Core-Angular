3rd Party Data Access

This console controls which authorized third party — a recruiting service, for example — may sign in and download your event's data: player rosters (player and parent names and emails) and the full event schedule. You manage it event by event: each row is one of your events with an open access window, and each event grants or withholds access on its own.

You are releasing data about minors to an outside agency.

Once downloaded, the data leaves your control. Review your organization's privacy policy before granting, and grant only to third parties your organization has actually agreed to work with.

What the third party can download

  • Rosters — released only for age groups you have flagged Third-Party Roster Access in the L-A-D-T Editor (Teams & Rosters). Unflagged age groups are never included.
  • Schedule — the event's full schedule is included whenever access is active.

Reading the table

Active

The listed login can sign in to this event and download now. The only action is Inactivate.

Inactive

This third party held access before but cannot sign in now. Re-Activate restores it in one click.

Not granted

No third party has access to this event yet. The row still names the third party that Grant access would authorize, so you can read it before you click.

Who you are granting to

Every row names the third party before you act — you never have to open a control to find out who is involved. The person's name is on the first line; their login, usually an email address, sits beneath it in a fixed-width font.

  • If your organization has authorized one third party — the usual case — that name appears on every row and Grant access authorizes it. There is nothing to choose.
  • If your organization has authorized more than one, the row lists them as radio buttons with every name visible at once. Choose one, then Grant access.

Granting and removing access

  • Grant access / Re-Activate take effect immediately — there is no extra confirmation step, so treat the click itself as the decision.
  • Inactivate ends the third party's ability to sign in to that event immediately. The history is kept, so you can Re-Activate later without setting anything up again.
  • Each event allows one active third party at a time. To switch providers, Inactivate the current one first, then grant the other.
  • Access window ends is the date the login expires on that event regardless of its Active state.

Why only one name (or none) is offered

This screen is reuse-only: it offers only third-party logins that have already been authorized for your organization at least once. It never creates a first-time login — that initial authorization is set up with your TSIC administrator. Afterward, you apply or remove that login across your events here, yourself. Most organizations work with a single recruiting service, so seeing one name repeated down the table is normal, not an error.

Common mistakes

  • Granting access but the third party sees no rosters. Rosters are released per age group — flag the intended age groups Third-Party Roster Access in the L-A-D-T Editor.
  • Expecting a new provider to appear on its own. First-time logins aren't created here; contact your TSIC administrator to authorize a new third party.
  • Reading a named row as an active grant. A row can name a third party and still be Not granted — the chip beside the name, not the name itself, tells you whether access is live.
  • Forgetting to Inactivate after an event's recruiting period. Access continues until you Inactivate it or the access window ends — whichever comes first.
