ARB Subscription Health

ARB Health watches over your event's Automatic Recurrent Billing — the families paying by installment plan. It surfaces the two things that break those payment plans before they cost you money: cards that are about to expire, and payments that have already been declined. From here you can email the affected families a ready-made message telling them exactly how to fix it.

This is your safety net for recurring revenue.

Every registrant on this page is one failed charge away from a gap in payment. Working the list keeps installment plans on track instead of quietly lapsing.

The page opens empty — that's on purpose

Nothing loads until you ask for it. Two buttons run the lookups:

  • Lookup Behind in Payment — families whose automatic payment was already declined and who now owe a balance. They need to update their card and clear what's due. This reads the subscription statuses already stored for your job, so it comes back quickly.
  • Lookup Expiring Cards — families whose card on file expires this month. Their next automatic payment will fail unless they update the card first. This one queries Authorize.Net live, so give it a moment.

Clicking the lookup you're already on re-runs it — that's how you refresh the list after sending emails or after a family fixes their card.

Refresh ARB Statuses

The button at the top of the page re-checks every subscription in the job against Authorize.Net and writes back the current status (active, suspended, expired, terminated, canceled). It's the one place those stored statuses get synced, and the Behind in Payment lookup is only as accurate as the last refresh.

  • Click it before your first lookup of the session so you're working from live status.
  • On a large job it can take a while — it checks one subscription at a time. Let it finish.
  • It reports Checked · updated · failed. "Updated" means the status changed since last time; "failed" means Authorize.Net didn't answer for that subscription — usually harmless, and the next refresh picks it up.
  • If a lookup was already on screen, the refresh reloads it automatically. If not, it leaves the page neutral — refreshing never runs a lookup for you.

Reading the list

  • Registrant — name, team/assignment, and the Family Username. That username is what the family logs in with, and the email templates quote it back to them, so it's the single most useful thing on the row when someone calls confused.
  • Status — the stored Authorize.Net subscription status. Green = active, amber = suspended, red = anything else (expired, terminated, unknown).
  • Fee Total and Paid — what the registration costs and what's come in so far.
  • Owes Total — the full remaining balance on the registration.
  • Owes Now — the part that is past due today based on the installment schedule. This is the number the collection templates quote. It shows red when it's above zero.
  • Next Payment and Progress — when the next installment is scheduled and how far through the plan they are (for example, "3 of 6").
  • Contact — the player's email plus both parent contacts. Emails and phone numbers are clickable; clicking one opens your mail or phone app without disturbing the row's checkbox.

Emailing flagged families

The two lookups deliberately work differently:

  • Expiring Cards is one click: Send Warning to All Registrants & Directors sends the standard expiration notice to everyone on the list. There's nothing to select and nothing to compose — it's the routine monthly warning.
  • Behind in Payment is select-and-compose, because collections messages usually need a human touch.
  1. Select the registrants to contact — click anywhere on a row, or use the header checkbox to take the whole list.
  2. Click Compose Email, then apply a template. Pick the one that matches the subscription status:
    • Active/Suspended Subscriptions — points them at "Update CC Info", which updates the card and clears the failed payments in one step.
    • Expired/Terminated Subscriptions — points them at "Pay Balance Due", because there's no live subscription left to repair.
  3. Edit the subject and body freely — the template is a starting point, not a locked form.
  4. Insert tokens where you want per-family values: !PLAYER, !OWEDNOW, !OWEDTOTAL, !FAMILYUSERNAME, !JOBLINK, and more. Click a token to drop it at your cursor.
  5. Leave "Email a summary to job directors" checked to send staff a record of who was contacted, then Send to N recipient(s). Sending runs in the background; a summary appears when it finishes, listing any addresses that failed.

Common mistakes

  • Working a stale list. Refresh ARB Statuses first — otherwise Behind in Payment reflects whatever the statuses were the last time anyone synced.
  • Waiting until payments fail. Run Expiring Cards early in the month — the whole point is to fix the card before the charge bounces.
  • Editing the fix-it steps out of the template. The numbered login steps are how families actually resolve it; keep them intact.
  • Sending with nothing selected. On Behind in Payment, emails go only to the rows you've checked — the Send button counts them for you, so confirm the count before you click.
  • Picking the wrong template. Telling a family with a terminated subscription to "update CC info" sends them looking for a menu item they no longer have.
