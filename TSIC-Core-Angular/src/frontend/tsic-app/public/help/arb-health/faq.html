ARB Subscription Health

Timing is everything on the Expiring Cards lookup.

A card flagged as expiring hasn't failed yet — you have a window to get it updated before the next automatic charge. Act early in the month, not after the decline.

What does ARB stand for?

Automatic Recurrent Billing — the installment payment plans families sign up for. This page monitors the health of those plans and flags the ones at risk of failing.

Why is the page empty when I open it?

By design. Nothing loads until you click a lookup button, so opening the page never fires off work you didn't ask for. Choose Lookup Behind in Payment or Lookup Expiring Cards to fill the grid.

What's the difference between the two lookups?

Expiring Cards is preventive — the card on file expires this month and the next charge will fail if it isn't updated. Behind in Payment is corrective — a charge already declined and the family now owes a balance. One is "fix it before," the other "fix it after."

What does "Refresh ARB Statuses" actually do?

It walks every subscription in the job, asks Authorize.Net for its current status, and stores the answer. That stored status is what the Behind in Payment lookup reads, so a refresh is what makes that list trustworthy. It's read-only at Authorize.Net — it can't charge, change, or cancel anything.

Run it at the start of a working session. On a big job it takes a while, since it checks one subscription at a time.

The refresh says "Checked 120 · updated 4 · 2 failed" — what's a failure?

Checked is how many subscriptions were looked at, updated how many had changed status since last time, and failed how many Authorize.Net didn't answer for. A couple of failures is normally a transient hiccup — those subscriptions simply keep their previous stored status and get picked up on the next refresh. A refresh where everything fails points at an Authorize.Net credential problem for the job, not at the families.

What's the difference between "Owes Now" and "Owes Total"?

Owes Total is everything still outstanding on the registration, including installments that aren't due yet. Owes Now is only the part that is past due today by the installment schedule — the amount you can fairly ask a family for right now. The collection templates quote Owes Now via the !OWEDNOW token.

Why does "Owes Now" read $0.00 on the Expiring Cards list?

Because nothing has failed yet. An expiring card is a warning about the next charge, not a missed one — so there's no past-due amount to show. If that family also has a genuine arrears balance, they'll turn up on the Behind in Payment lookup with a real figure.

A family I know is behind isn't on the list. Why?

Three common reasons, all deliberate:

  • 48-hour grace. If their scheduled payment date was less than two days ago, they're held back — the charge may still be settling, and nobody wants a dunning email over a payment that's about to post.
  • Canceled subscriptions are skipped. A canceled plan isn't a billing failure to chase here; handle that balance through the registrant's accounting.
  • Stale status. Click Refresh ARB Statuses and run the lookup again.
Which email template should I pick?

Match it to the Status column. Active/Suspended Subscriptions sends them to "Update CC Info", which repairs the card and clears the failed payments in one step — that only works while a subscription still exists. Expired/Terminated Subscriptions sends them to "Pay Balance Due" instead, because there's no live plan left to fix.

If your selection mixes both kinds, send in two passes — one template per group.

Is there help for parents, and where do I send them?

Yes. Most of the time you won't need to send them anywhere — the email templates on this page already carry the step-by-step instructions to the parent. Reach for the help page when someone phones you.

Families have their own written help in two places, both behind the ? button:

The Update Payment Method screen
For a parent already on the page
The player registration screens
For a parent who doesn't know where to start

Between them they cover:

  • Signing in, choosing the player's role, and finding Update CC Info in the User Menu.
  • What Balance Due at the top of the form means — zero is up to date, anything else is charged when they submit.
  • That updating the card and paying a missed installment are the same single step.
  • That each player carries their own card, so a family with two players repeats it under Switch Role.
  • Browser, device, and firewall troubleshooting when the form won't go through.
The call you'll actually get: "I don't see Update CC Info."

That item only appears for a player who is on an automatic-payment plan. Nine times out of ten the parent is signed in under a different player — have them use Switch Role and look again.

Can I edit a template before sending?

Yes — applying a template just pre-fills the subject and body, and both are fully editable. Keep the numbered login steps intact, though: they're the part families actually act on.

What are the tokens like !PLAYER and !OWEDNOW?

Personalization placeholders. When the email goes out, each token is replaced with that recipient's real value. Click one to insert it at your cursor. The full set:

  • !PLAYER — the player's name (!PERSON works too, if that's the habit you have from the Search composer)
  • !FAMILYUSERNAME — the username the family logs in with
  • !JOBLINK — the event name as a clickable link; !JOBNAME for the name alone
  • !OWEDNOW — past-due amount; !OWEDTOTAL — full remaining balance
  • !FEETOTAL and !PAIDTOTAL — total fees and total paid
  • !SUBSCRIPTIONID and !SUBSCRIPTIONSTATUS — the Authorize.Net subscription details
What does "email a summary to job directors" send?

One recap message to the job's directors listing the families that were contacted — not a copy of each individual email, and not a BCC. Leave it on so staff have a record of the outreach; turn it off only for a deliberately quiet send.

The one-click "Send Warning to All" — who gets it?

Everyone currently listed on the Expiring Cards lookup, plus a summary to directors, using the standard expiration notice. It's all-or-nothing by design — that list is the routine monthly warning. There's no per-recipient selection or custom wording on that side; if you need to say something different, contact the family directly using the links in the Contact column.

What happens after I click Send?

The batch runs in the background and the button stays busy until it's done. You then get a summary — how many sent, how many failed, and the specific addresses that failed. A failed address usually means a bad or blocked email; the E-Mail Troubleshooter page will tell you which.

Why is the family username shown on every row?

Because "which username do I log in with?" is the question that stalls these fixes. Every template quotes it back to the family automatically, and having it on the row means you can answer a phone call without digging.

Clicking a row selects it — how do I just email or call a parent?

Click the email address or phone number itself in the Contact column. Those links open your mail or phone app and deliberately don't toggle the row's checkbox, so your selection stays intact.

Tip: make ARB Health a monthly habit — refresh statuses, run Expiring Cards near the start of the month, then run Behind in Payment. A few minutes there prevents most of the declines you'd otherwise be chasing later.
