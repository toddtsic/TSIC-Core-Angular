Administrators

Family and player logins can never hold admin roles.

Family credentials are shared within the household. The search won't offer them and the server refuses them — this is deliberate, not a bug.

How do I add a brand-new administrator who has no account?

Have them register on this job's site as a coach/staff adult — that creates a personal account. They'll then show up in this screen's search badged Pending adult. Accept them with the role you want; their pending registration is converted into the admin registration and they leave the coach-approval queue automatically.

Why can't I find the person I'm searching for?

The search only offers eligible accounts: existing admin-only accounts, or pending coach/staff adults registered with this customer. If the person only has a family or player login, they won't appear — have them create their own coach/staff account on the site first. If they registered with a different customer, they need to register on this site.

A parent should be our new Director. Can I use their family account?

No — and the screen will refuse it. Their family login stays with the household (the kids may know the password). Have the parent register on the site as a coach/staff adult with their own username and email, then accept that account here. Two logins, cleanly separated lives.

What happens to their coach registration when I accept a Pending adult?

It becomes the admin registration — same row, converted in place. They drop out of the coach-approval queue and won't be granted teams as a coach. If someone should genuinely be both a coach and an admin, talk it through first: those are two different access lives and usually deserve two accounts.

What does the star do?

Marks the job's primary contact — the Director published as the event's point of contact. One per job. Click to set; click the starred row again to clear.

Should I deactivate or delete a departing admin?

Deactivate if they might return (between seasons) — the row stays, access stops. Delete when they're gone for good. Either way, do it promptly — stale admin access is the easiest thing to forget and the most expensive to leave open.
