Manual ARB Sweep

Every morning at 4 AM a background pass reads Authorize.Net's settled batches and reconciles them against our books. This screen runs that same pass on demand. It is the only manual entry point to it, and it is SuperUser‑only.

What this button does depends entirely on which server you are on.

On production it books money and emails families. Anywhere else it only looks and reports. You do not choose — the server does, and the banner at the top of the page tells you which one you are looking at before you click.

The seven things the sweep does

  • Imports recurring (ARB) charges — each installment that drafted overnight is booked against the registration, and the subscription's status is re-synced from Authorize.Net.
  • Stamps eCheck settlements — status only. eCheck money books when it is submitted; this records that the draft actually entered the banking network.
  • Reverses eCheck returns — an NSF or closed account comes back days later, and the booked payment is backed out.
  • Flags orphan charges — money that settled at Authorize.Net with no matching accounting row here. Reported for a human, never auto-booked. Expect zero.
  • Runs the stale-draft watchdog — a draft that went silent instead of settling or returning. This is the only detector for one that died before it ever reached the bank.
  • Reports untracked eCheck payments — review only. Expect zero.
  • Emails the family behind every recurring draft that failed. This is the newest step and the reason the dry run exists.

The failed-draft notice

When a family's installment is declined, Authorize.Net keeps retrying, so the subscription stays active — which meant these failures never showed up in the old warning lists. Nobody was told. Now the family gets an email the same morning, telling them which payment failed, what is owed, and the exact steps to fix it.

Which message they get depends on whether their plan is still alive:

Plan still alive

“Update Your Payment Information” — update the card and clear the balance in one step, which also restarts their future automatic payments.

Plan already ended

“Pay Balance Due” — there is no live plan left to repair, so they are sent straight to paying what they owe.

Some families cannot be written to — no address on file, no username to give them, or they have opted out of email. Those are never guessed at: they are listed by name, on screen and in the digest, for someone to phone.

Live vs. dry run

The mode follows the server and cannot be switched. That is deliberate: a checkbox's only real ability is to be in the wrong state, and the wrong state here is a morning of payments silently not booked.

Dry run — every server except production

Reads the real production Authorize.Net batches and works out exactly what the 4 AM pass would do — then does none of it. Nothing is booked, nothing is settled, no email is sent. Instead the digest and every would-be family email are shown on screen for you to read.

Live — production only

The real pass. Books charges, settles and reverses eChecks, emails families, mails the digest to support. There is no undo. The banner is red and the button says Run sweep (LIVE).

Reading a dry-run result

Counts Who was missed The emails The digest
  • Checked — transactions in the batches for the window you asked for.
  • ARB Resolved — recurring transactions worked out in full. On a live run this label reads ARB Imported, because there it also books them.
  • Failed Drafts / Would Email / NOT Emailed — the three that matter. Found minus emailed is the number of families a person still has to contact by hand.
  • eCheck Settled, eCheck Returns, Orphans — hidden on a dry run. Those steps do not run at all, so showing zeros would only invite you to read them as good news.

Below the counts: the by-name list of families nobody could reach, then every rendered email — click one to expand it and see the recipients, the Reply-To director and the full message as the family would receive it — and finally the digest as support would get it.

The expiring-card pass

A second, separate job runs unattended on the 2nd and the 15th of each month. It asks Authorize.Net which cards expire this month across every job holding a live subscription, and writes to those families before the payment fails. It has its own summary to support.

On a dry-run server you get a button for it. On production you do not, and that is on purpose: it mails thousands of people, it is scheduled precisely so nobody has to decide to send it, and a manual trigger would only be a way to send it twice or on the wrong day.

Not the 1st — that morning belongs to the month-end close alone.

Days prior

How far back to ask Authorize.Net for settled batches. 1 day matches the nightly cadence and is the right answer almost always. Raise it to catch up after an outage, or to look at a window you think was missed. Already-processed transactions are recognised and skipped, so a wider window costs time, not correctness.

Only one sweep runs at a time.

If a pass is already in flight the request is refused rather than queued, and you will see “Sweep already running”. Wait and try again.
