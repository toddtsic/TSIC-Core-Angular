Pro tips

The invitation tools come first — everything after them is organized by where you find it on screen. Click any title to expand it.

Send Invitations

This screen can email a personalized, expiring invitation link to everyone your search returns — no link-building, no spreadsheet. There are three kinds, and you never choose between them: there is only ever one Invite button, and it renames itself to say which kind your search and your event qualify for.

You will see one of these — never two, never a choice between them.

Two conditions apply to all three. Miss either one and the button is simply not there:

  • Your search is narrowed to exactly one role — Players or Club Reps. Leave Role on "All", or tick two roles, and no invitation is possible.
  • Your search is filtered to Active only. Invitations never go to dropped or inactive registrations, so the button stays hidden until you've excluded them.
Don't see an Invite button?

One of the conditions isn't met. Check the two above first — they account for most cases — then the extra condition listed under whichever invitation you're trying to send. Nothing on this screen announces which one is missing; the button just doesn't appear.

Your Role filter has four qualifying choices, and both variants of each role work the same way for invitations:

Player Player (NOT WAITLISTED) Club Rep Club Rep (ACTIVE, NOT WAITLISTED)
The link token is the invitation — never delete it.

Each letter below arrives with a !TOKEN where the personal link goes. Reword everything around it as much as you like, but if you delete the token itself, nothing stops the send — the batch simply goes out as an ordinary email with no link in it, and every recipient gets an invitation they cannot act on. There is no warning and no way to recall it. The composer lists the token your letter needs; keep whichever it names.

Invite Tryout Players Who Made The Cut

What it does. Emails players from a finished event a personal link to register for your next one — the tryout-to-season handoff, without asking anyone to re-find your site or re-type their details. It works between any two of your events, not just tryouts: camp to league, clinic to season, all the same.

What has to be true:

  • Role filtered to Players (either Player choice).
  • Status filtered to Active only.
  • This event is past its user-expiry date. Invitations are a post-event tool — while the event you're looking at is still open to its own users, there is no Invite button for players at all.
  • At least one other event under your organization has Allow Player Registration switched on and hasn't expired. Without a destination there is nothing to invite anyone to, so the button stays hidden.

Turning the destination on. That switch lives on the receiving event, not this one — switch to that event, then Configure Job → Player:

Allow Player Registration
Player registration is enabled — families can register players.
Require Invitation Token
Open registration — any player can register without an invite.
"Require Invitation Token" is optional — and it's the one that makes an invitation exclusive.

Leave it off and your invitation is a convenience: it takes players straight in, but anyone else can still register normally. Switch it on and the invite link becomes the only way into that event — which is what you want for an invitation-only season built from tryout results. Either way the Invite button works the same; this only controls who else can get in. It stays greyed out until Allow Player Registration is on.

→ tick 3 rows →

Nothing ticked sends to every player your search found, including rows on pages you never scrolled to. Tick rows and it narrows to exactly those.

The composer then asks for the destination and how long the links stay good. It defaults to 24 hours; every recipient in the batch gets the same deadline:

The letter it writes for you — edit the wording freely, but leave the !TOKENS alone:

Subject: You're invited to register for !EVENT_INVITEDTO

Hi !PERSON,

You've been invited to register for !EVENT_INVITEDTO. Use your personalized link below:

!INVITE_LINK

This invitation is unique to you and expires on !INVITE_EXPIRES. Please complete your registration before then.

  • !PERSON → the recipient's name
  • !EVENT_INVITEDTO → the target event you picked above
  • !INVITE_LINK → that player's own single-use registration link
  • !INVITE_EXPIRES → the exact date and time, set by the dropdown
Deleting !INVITE_LINK empties the invitation — it does not stop it.

The batch still sends, with no link in it. Change the sentence around the token as much as you like; keep the token itself.

Invite Club Reps to Preview Schedule

What it does. Lets your club reps see the game schedule before you release it to the public — so conflicts surface while you can still fix them quietly. Each rep sees it only through their own login, and only for this event.

What has to be true:

  • Role filtered to Club Reps (either Club Rep choice).
  • Status filtered to Active only.
  • Allow Club Rep Schedule Preview is switched on, under Configure Job → Scheduling.
  • Allow Public Schedule Access is switched off, same tab. If the schedule is already public there is nothing to preview, and the preview switch does nothing.
  • This event is still open to its users — the opposite of the other two invitations.

Turning it on. Unlike the other two invitations, both switches are on this event — no switching jobs. Go to Configure Job → Scheduling; they sit together at the top of the tab, each with a live description underneath so you can confirm the state at a glance. Tick Allow Club Rep Schedule Preview, leave Allow Public Schedule Access unticked, and save:

Allow Public Schedule Access
The game schedule is hidden from the public.
Allow Club Rep Schedule Preview
Club reps you invite can preview the schedule before it is released to the public.
Turning the preview switch off shuts every preview at once — including links you already sent. It's the kill switch if a schedule changes mid-review.

What you'll see when the search is right:

There's no destination to choose here — the preview is for the event you're already in, and the composer fills it in for you.

The letter it writes for you. It spells out the login steps, because a rep who clicks the link and picks the wrong role sees no schedule and no explanation:

Subject: Preview the schedule for !EVENT_INVITEDTO

Hi !PERSON,

The schedule for !EVENT_INVITEDTO is not public yet, and you're invited to preview it.

To see it:

  1. Click your personal link below.
  2. Log in with your username: !USERNAME
  3. When asked to choose a role, select Club Rep for !EVENT_INVITEDTO. Any other role will not show you this schedule.

!SCHEDULE_PREVIEW_LINK

This link is unique to you and works only with your login. View by !INVITE_EXPIRES.

  • !PERSON → the recipient's name
  • !USERNAME → that club rep's login username
  • !EVENT_INVITEDTO → this event's name
  • !SCHEDULE_PREVIEW_LINK → that rep's own preview link
  • !INVITE_EXPIRES → the exact date and time, set by the dropdown
Deleting !SCHEDULE_PREVIEW_LINK empties the invitation — it does not stop it.

The batch still sends, with no link in it. Keep the token; reword everything around it.

Keep step 3 in the letter.

The link opens the schedule only for this rep's own login and their Club Rep role on this event. Any other login, or the right login under a different role, quietly shows nothing — which reads to the rep as a broken link. The seeded wording is what prevents that call.

Invite Club Reps to Next Year's Event

What it does. Emails last season's club reps a personal link to register a team for your next event — so returning clubs can claim their place before open enrollment, and you can see who's coming back.

What has to be true:

  • Role filtered to Club Reps (either Club Rep choice).
  • Status filtered to Active only.
  • This event is past its user-expiry date, exactly as for player invitations.
  • At least one other event under your organization has Allow Team Registration switched on and hasn't expired. An event taking players but not teams won't appear as a choice.

Turning the destination on. As with player invitations, the switch is on the receiving event — switch to it, then Configure Job → Teams:

Allow Team Registration
Required — this is what puts the event in your target list.
Require Club Rep Invitation Token
Optional — on means invited reps only.
Use "Require Club Rep Invitation Token" to run a returning-clubs-only window.

Switch it on and only reps holding an invite link can register a team — so you can give last season's clubs first claim, then switch it off to open the event to everyone. Off, the invitation still works; it just isn't exclusive.

This and the schedule preview above are opposites, and can never both be available. While your event is live you can invite reps to preview the schedule; once it has expired you can invite them to next year's event. That's why the button's wording changes over the life of an event — it isn't a fault.

→ tick 2 rows →

Pick the event they're being invited to, and the window the links stay good for:

You've been invited to register your club/team for !EVENT_INVITEDTO. Use your personalized link below:

!CLUBREP_INVITE_LINK

This invitation is unique to you and expires on !INVITE_EXPIRES. Please complete your registration before then.

  • !PERSON → the recipient's name
  • !EVENT_INVITEDTO → the target event you picked above
  • !CLUBREP_INVITE_LINK → that rep's own single-use registration link

Main view

Email All vs Email Selected
  • Email All targets every registration matching your search — including rows on pages you never scrolled to. The audience is re-resolved from your filter criteria at send time, so the headline count is the real total, not the visible page.
  • Email Selected is exactly the rows you've checked — and checks accumulate across pages. Check three people on page 1, page ahead, check two more: the button reads "Email Selected (5)" and the composer lists all five by name.
Pre-built email templates — the list adapts to your search

The batch composer offers ready-made letters, but which ones appear depends on the job's features and what you searched. Scope the search to "ARB Behind — Active/Suspended" and the matching collections letter shows up, pre-written with the right instructions:

Update CC Info (Active/Suspended)
ARB — Behind in Payment
Pay Balance Due (Expired/Terminated)
ARB — Behind in Payment
Tip: if a template you expect is missing, your search probably isn't scoped to its audience yet — the picker only offers letters that fit the people in your results.
Email Tokens

Anywhere you compose — the batch composer here, or a single registrant's Email tab — you can insert tokens that render as each recipient's real values at send time:

!PERSON !EMAIL !FAMILYUSERNAME !JOBNAME !JOBLINK !AMTFEES !AMTPAID !AMTOWED !SEASON !SPORT !CUSTOMERNAME

So "Your balance of !AMTOWED for !JOBNAME" personalizes itself for every recipient in one send. Events with recurring billing or USA Lacrosse validation get extra tokens (subscription ID and status, membership valid-through date) — they appear in the token list only where they apply.

Filter chips

Every active filter shows as a removable chip above the results — what you see is always exactly what's applied:

Role: Player × Status: Active × Agegroup: 2027 ×

Tree selections stay tidy: checking an agegroup covers every division and team under it but shows one chip, not fifty. Removing that chip un-checks the whole subtree in one click.

Search for CC-Expiring Registrants

In events with recurring billing, the ARB section has a button that isn't a filter — it's a live lookup at the card processor:

results arrive under a dedicated chip: ARB: CCs Expiring This Month (12) ×

It deliberately ignores your filters — even dropped or inactive registrants surface if their card is about to fail — so you can collect before an automatic payment bounces. The × on the chip exits the lookup and restores your normal filters untouched.

Registrant detail panel

Click any name to open the panel. It's divided into three tabs — money first, identity second, correspondence third:

Accounting tab: refund, re-send confirmation, manage subscription
  • Process a credit-card refund — refund directly from the accounting ledger; the record lands in the same ledger you're looking at.
  • Re-Send Confirmation Email — one click re-sends the original registration confirmation. It's role-aware: for a player it sends the family confirmation to the family account.
  • Manage the recurring-billing subscription — for ARB registrants the subscription card shows payment progress and can pull the live status from the processor on demand; a live, active subscription can be cancelled right here.
